# Layout and QA

## Layout recipe

Use this recipe for an object-first cover unless the photo forces a different crop:

| Element | Placement | Job |
|---|---|---|
| Main object | lower centre, 45–60% of canvas | the evidence and visual anchor |
| Main headline | upper third, left-aligned or centered | the first thing legible in the feed |
| Category tag | top-left or near headline | quick topic classification |
| Function sticker | lower-right or beside the object | compact proof of what it does |
| Doodles | around empty negative space | eye guidance only |

Keep a clear margin near every edge. Avoid placing key copy over bright screens, small ports, or the item’s most distinctive feature.

## Subject routing examples

| Post premise | Best anchor | Why |
|---|---|---|
| “I rebuilt this ESP32/M5Stack” | object-first | the device is the story and proof |
| “This coffee and pastry made my Saturday” | object-first | the food is the sensory focus; keep its real colour and texture |
| “My desk workflow changed after this build” | object + hand or object-first | interaction may clarify use, but the object remains the proof |
| “I tested three voice-control workflows” | screenshot-first or object + screenshot | the result is demonstrated by the interface |
| “A day making this device” | person-first | creator experience is the story |

## Text and factual QA

Before delivery, verify each item:

- The headline exactly matches the approved wording and is legible at thumbnail scale.
- Brand, project, and tool names are spelled exactly as supplied.
- Labels describe confirmed functions only; no benchmark, latency, compatibility, price, or revenue claim is invented.
- No fake logo, QR code, watermark, account handle, or fabricated UI appears.
- There is one obvious visual anchor, not equal-weight title, device, screenshot, and decoration competing at once.
- The product, food, or object is recognizably the same item as in the user’s original photo.
- The final PNG is 1080 × 1440 px (or the exact platform size requested).

## Quick readability check

View the finished cover at a small scale. If the title cannot be read first, shorten it, increase contrast, or remove a label. If the object cannot be identified second, enlarge it or reduce decoration. If both work, stop—do not add more visual elements just because there is spare space.
